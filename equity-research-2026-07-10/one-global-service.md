## 1. Executive Summary

**Verdict: AVOID** | Conviction: 25/100 | Risk: 80/100

**Key Thesis (Bull Case):** ONEGLOBAL has engineered a dramatic transformation from a dormant synthetic-fibres shell into a reported healthcare/diagnostics services company. Revenue went from ₹3.67 Cr (FY21) to ₹498 Cr (FY26) — a 5-year CAGR of ~167%. ROCE is 87%, ROE 65%, debt is near-zero, and the stock trades at just 17x TTM earnings — a significant discount to diagnostic peers at 55–80x. PlusCare International merger (completed March 2025 via NCLT) appears to have injected a real diagnostics engine. On paper, the metrics are spectacular.

**Key Bear Thesis (Why to Avoid):** The forensic risk profile for this stock is extreme. This is a company with a dormant/sick history until ~2019, controlled by promoters who acquired a near-zero-revenue shell and then reported 167% revenue CAGR. The revenue jump is overwhelmingly merger-driven (PlusCare contributed the bulk of the FY26 surge). There is zero mutual fund participation, near-zero DII interest, only 3 FII investors. Auditor is a small unknown firm (M/s SDPM & Co.). Segment-wise revenue breakdown is not disclosed. Customer concentration is unknown. Cash flow from operations (₹14.45 Cr in FY25) is starkly divergent from reported PAT (₹18.46 Cr in FY25) — a major red flag. FY26 OCF not confirmed. A SEBI clarification notice was issued October 2025. The company was "sick," "dormant," and carried out no operational activity through 2012. The 2019 promoter change brought new faces with limited public track record in healthcare. Revenue of ₹498 Cr from a ₹7.10 Cr paid-up capital company with ₹255 Cr in total assets strains credibility without cleaner revenue breakdowns.

**3–5 Year Outlook:** Highly uncertain. If PlusCare revenues are real and scalable, the company could be a mid-cap healthcare services compounder. If the revenue is inflated, related-party-sourced, or non-recurring, significant downside to book (~₹72/share) is possible.

**Biggest Unknowns:**
1. True revenue composition and customer names — not publicly disclosed
2. Nature and sustainability of ₹498 Cr revenue (which segments, which clients)
3. FY26 operating cash flow vs. PAT (cash-earnings quality)
4. Promoter's actual business background and how PlusCare was funded
5. What the SEBI October 2025 clarification notice pertained to

**Margin of Safety:** Negative (at 17x PE of potentially low-quality earnings with extreme governance risk)

**Confidence Level: LOW-MEDIUM** (financial metrics confirmed; business substance and earnings quality unverified)

## 15. Short-Seller Attack

**The Bear Case Narrative (adversarial perspective):**

1. **Classic shell-company revival pattern.** Overseas Synthetics was incorporated in 1992, became a "sick industrial company" by 2002, carried out zero operational activity by 2011-12. The previous promoter group then sold to the Dhawangale family in 2019 for approximately ₹50 lakhs total. A synthetic fibres shell company was renamed "One Global Service Provider" in 2020 — the name alone signals aspirational branding with no operational basis.

2. **Revenue acceleration exceeds credibility of asset base.** A company with ₹1.79 Cr net fixed assets and ₹255 Cr total assets (essentially all current — likely trade receivables/cash) is claiming ₹498 Cr revenue. Legitimate healthcare services businesses require labs, equipment, staff, and infrastructure. The near-absence of tangible assets is inconsistent with real diagnostic/lab revenue at this scale.

3. **OCF/PAT divergence is the smoking gun.** FY24: PAT ₹7.11 Cr, OCF ₹1.21 Cr (17% cash conversion). This pattern — profit on paper, minimal cash — is the hallmark of channel stuffing, related-party billing, or other revenue inflation techniques common in Indian small-cap frauds (see: Vakrangee, Manpasand, Kwality, etc.). FY25 recovered (78%) but FY26 data not yet confirmed.

4. **Unknown auditor for a ₹1,200 Cr company.** M/s SDPM & Co. is an obscure firm auditing a company that claims ₹498 Cr revenue and carries a ₹1,200 Cr market cap. In genuine fraud cases (Satyam excluded), small auditors are often chosen precisely because they do not probe deeply. No Big-4 or even ICAI-recognized mid-tier firm is involved.

5. **Zero institutional confidence is a verdict.** Dr. Lal PathLabs at 55x P/E and Vijaya Diagnostic at 82x P/E attract mutual funds. ONEGLOBAL at 17x P/E with stated 65% ROE has literally zero mutual fund participation. The market is not stupid — institutions have looked and passed. The only buyers are retail and a handful of FII entities (possibly related parties or operators).

6. **SEBI notice October 2025.** A SEBI clarification notice was issued in October 2025 for a company with 8,461 shareholders and ₹1,200 Cr market cap. The details are not in the public domain. This is a material disclosure that deserves full transparency. Its absence from major financial news coverage is itself suspicious.

7. **Director resignation February 2026.** Harshal Dubali resigned as additional director in February 2026. No reason was given. In a company with governance concerns, unexplained senior exits are a leading indicator.

8. **The PlusCare acquisition mechanism deserves scrutiny.** PlusCare International Private Limited was announced as an acquisition for ₹42 Cr in February 2023. By March 2025, it was merged into ONEGLOBAL via NCLT. The FY26 revenue of ₹498 Cr vs. FY25 ₹147 Cr suggests PlusCare contributes ₹300+ Cr. Where was PlusCare's revenue in prior years? If it had ₹300 Cr revenue when acquired for ₹42 Cr, that was a remarkable bargain — or the revenue appeared only post-merger via related-party arrangements.

9. **Circuit filter risk.** The stock has only BSE listing with ~₹40L average daily traded value. A SEBI action, adverse media, or promoter selling event could trigger consecutive lower circuit days with no exit possible for any meaningful holder. Price discovery in Indian micro-caps with retail dominance and illiquidity is notoriously asymmetric.
