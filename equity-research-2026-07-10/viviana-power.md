## PART 1 — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Verdict: HOLD (leaning Avoid for fresh institutional allocation)**
**Conviction: 30/100 | Risk: 72/100**

**Key Thesis:** Viviana Power Tech is a high-growth, Gujarat-concentrated power EPC contractor with 16x revenue growth since its 2022 SME listing. The promoter (MD Nikesh Choksi, 34 years ex-GETCO/Torrent/Adani Transmission) has genuine domain expertise. The company is riding a real secular capex wave — India's National Electricity Plan mandates ₹9.15 lakh crore in transmission investment by 2032, and Gujarat is a leading implementation state. The company recently secured ₹1,000+ crore order book and is now entering transformer manufacturing and BESS (battery energy storage), potentially transitioning from pure-play EPC contractor to vertically integrated power platform.

**Key Bear Thesis:** Despite stellar reported profits, the company has never generated meaningful free cash flow (FCF consistently negative: FY24 -₹4 Cr, FY25 -₹3 Cr, FY26 -₹1 Cr). Receivables days ballooned from 153 to 245 over 3 years. OCF was +₹1 Cr in FY26 vs PAT of ₹50 Cr — a severe cash-earnings quality gap. The ₹2,448 Cr MoU with Gujarat Government is non-binding intent, not a firm order. The ₹100 Cr transformer capex is being funded by NCDs at 12% coupon — costly debt for an EPC business with thin structural free cash margins. At 65 employees executing ₹533 Cr revenue, human capital concentration is extreme. The SME-to-mainboard migration removed circuit-filter protections and increases daily volatility.

**3–5 Year Outlook:** Favorable macro (power capex supercycle) supports order intake. If the company successfully executes BESS projects, manages receivables, and delivers transformer revenue by FY28-30, it could grow to ₹1,500–2,000 Cr revenue by FY30 with improving margins. BUT: the transformation from EPC to manufacturing is a capability and capital jump that many small EPC companies fail at. Execution, working capital, and margin risk are HIGH.

**Biggest Unknowns:**
1. Actual cash collection rate on ₹300+ Cr receivables (60%+ was uncollected as of May 2026)
2. Whether MoU of ₹2,448 Cr converts to firm orders (MoUs in Gujarat Vibrant are frequently non-binding)
3. BESS execution capability (no prior track record in energy storage)
4. Transformer manufacturing success (greenfield, new technology domain, ₹100 Cr capex starting FY27)
5. Debt trajectory: NCD issuance at 12% in three tranches risks D/E exceeding 1.5x

**Confidence in Assessment: MEDIUM** — data is available for most financial metrics but forensic-level audit reports, RPT details, and cash collection verification are not publicly accessible.

## PART 3 — INDUSTRY & SECTOR

### Sector Overview
India Power Transmission & Distribution EPC is a structurally high-growth sector driven by:
- **National Electricity Plan (NEP) 2032:** ₹9.15 lakh crore transmission investment target
- **RDSS (Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme):** ₹3.03 lakh crore budgeted; 4.76 Cr smart meters installed to date
- **Renewable Energy Evacuation:** 280 GW by 2030 requires massive transmission build-out (335 GW ISTS capacity)
- **Green Energy Corridors Phase-II:** 10,750 ckm of intra-state lines
- **BESS Policy:** National storage mandate supports battery storage project awards

### Market Size & Growth
- India T&D EPC market: USD 14.68 Bn (2025) → USD 35.20 Bn (2035); **CAGR ~9.3%** (Source: Astute Analytica, Jan 2026)
- Western India (Gujarat) is largest regional contributor at 31.6% of market revenue
- Order books for all T&D EPC players are at multi-year highs

### Government Policy Relevance
- **RDSS:** Direct beneficiary; underground cabling, smart grid, loss reduction — Viviana's core Gujarat DISCOM work falls here (HIGH impact)
- **TBCB (Tariff Based Competitive Bidding):** Larger projects; Viviana not yet empaneled for 765 kV TBCB (unlike Bajel) — limits addressable market in biggest tenders
- **PLI Scheme:** Not directly relevant to EPC contracting; relevant to transformer manufacturing (future)
- **BESS VGF (Viability Gap Funding):** Enables BESS project economics; Viviana RRVUNL project benefits

### Porter's Five Forces

| Buyer power | HIGH | State utilities dictate terms; slow payment; re-tender every contract |

| Substitutes | LOW | Infrastructure physical assets; no substitute for transmission lines |
| Competitive rivalry | HIGH | KEC, Kalpataru, Bajel, L&T, many regional players; margin pressure endemic |

**Cyclicality:** MEDIUM — tied to government capex cycles, but India's power capex is now multi-year committed. Short-term risk exists if RDSS disbursement delays.

**Expected Sector CAGR (Relevant for Viviana):** 12-15% revenue CAGR for mid-sized T&D EPC players (above sector average due to low base and market share gains).
