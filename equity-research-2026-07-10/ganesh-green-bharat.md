Ganesh Green Bharat operates across three interlocked verticals:

1. **Solar PV Module Manufacturing** — 1.1 GW facility in Mehsana, Gujarat. Products: Mono PERC, TOPCon N-type (G12R), HJT. Operating at ~69-75% utilisation as of H1 FY26. Plans to expand to 2 GW by FY27-28.

## 3. Industry & Sector

### 3.1 India Solar Market Context
India surpassed 157 GW installed solar capacity by early 2026, making it the world's second-largest solar growth market. The government targets 500 GW renewable capacity by 2030. Annual additions running at ~40-50 GW/year demand sustained EPC and manufacturing activity.

**Source:** Multiple industry publications (reslink.org, tenderdekho.com).

### 3.2 Government Policy Tailwinds — Direct Relevance to GGBL
- **PLI Scheme (Solar PV):** ₹4,500 Cr scheme across two tranches supporting 48+ GW of domestic manufacturing. GGBL is BIS certified — eligible for domestic preference in government tenders (ALMM list). GGBL has NOT publicly disclosed receiving PLI benefit directly (requires verification).
- **Basic Customs Duty (BCD):** 40% duty on imported modules makes Indian-manufactured modules preferred in government tenders — GGBL directly benefits.
- **BESS VGF Scheme:** ₹91 Bn Viability Gap Funding for 43.2 GWh of BESS capacity (up to 40% capex support). NTPC REL is a major BESS developer using VGF — GGBL's anchor client is thus backed by government money. Strong structural tailwind.
- **Round-the-Clock (RTC) and FDRE Tenders:** Now mandate co-located storage, creating a structural BESS EPC market.

**Source:** MNRE (mnre.gov.in), IEEFA (ieefa.org), SolarQuarter.

### 3.3 Market Size
- Solar EPC market: USD 8-12 Bn (~₹67,000-1,00,000 Cr) in 2026; 12-16% CAGR through 2035.
- BESS market: India added only 547 MWh in all of 2025 (Mercom); now accelerating. Several GWh/year expected by 2027-28.

**Confidence in TAM estimates: MEDIUM-LOW** — sources are market research firms; actual addressable market for a sub-₹1,000 Cr company is far smaller.

## 8. Government Exposure

Ganesh Green Bharat is substantially a government-revenue business. Essentially ALL identified customers are government entities: NTPC REL, Goa Shipyard, state utilities, government solar schemes. The unnamed ₹430 Cr module customer (April 2026) is the only potential private/independent IPP customer — its identity is not disclosed.

**Policy exposures:**
- **Positive:** BCD on imported modules; PLI scheme; BESS VGF; PM-KUSUM (solar pumps); Har Ghar Bijli; RDSS (distribution sector reform) — all increase demand for GGBL's services.
- **Negative:** Government payment delays are endemic in Indian infrastructure (a major reason EPC companies have poor cash conversion). Any budget reallocation, policy change on BESS support, or delay in NTPC project execution directly hits GGBL.
- **Budget exposure:** A significant share of GGBL's addressable market flows through central and state government capital budgets. Revenue is therefore correlated with government capex cycles — elections, budget cuts, or state financial stress can cause sudden order pauses.

**Confidence in government exposure assessment: HIGH** — confirmed by DRHP risk factors and customer identity from news.

## 15. Short-Seller Attack / Bear Case

*Assuming this is a value trap or operator-driven SME. Evidence-based case:*

**1. The Classic SME IPO Pump:**
- IPO at ₹190 → listing at ~₹370 (+95%) → peak ₹555 → now ₹261. The pattern is textbook: high GMP pre-listing, circuit-limit driven price discovery on listing day, rapid ascent to peak, then slow bleed. FIIs and DIIs who got allocation sold into the rally. Retail holders are left.

**2. Institutional Exit is the Tell:**
FIIs exited 100% of their holding and DIIs 87% of their holding within 18 months of listing. These are professional investors with annual report access, management meetings, and forensic diligence. Their exit speaks louder than any financial metric.

**3. Cash Earnings are Not Real:**
₹75 Cr PAT; ₹21 Cr OCF; ₹6 Cr FCF. In 5 years, cumulative FCF is approximately -₹52 Cr while cumulative PAT is approximately ₹140 Cr. The business has never generated meaningful cash at the ownership level. Revenue recognition in EPC contracts (% completion method) can be managed; cash cannot.

**4. The BESS Pivot is Unverifiable:**
₹1,510 Cr of a ₹2,210 Cr order book (68%) is BESS. GGBL has zero disclosed completed BESS projects. The NTPC REL order (1,000 MWh) was announced June 2026 — if this single order represents most of the BESS book, the company has essentially ONE large untested project. The order value is not disclosed (a red flag for a company that announces ₹6 Cr Goa Shipyard contracts with fanfare).

**5. Customer Concentration — Unnamed ₹430 Cr Module Customer:**
The largest disclosed single order (₹430 Cr, April 2026) has an undisclosed customer ("domestic"). For a company with transparent governance, large arm's-length contracts would be disclosed. This raises a low-probability but non-zero concern about related-party revenue.

**6. Margin Trajectory is Wrong Direction:**
If GGBL were building a moat through scale, margins should expand. Instead, EBITDA margin went 8.5% → 15.6% → 20.6% → 15.1% → 10.7%. The peak was FY24 at ₹170 Cr revenue — a smaller company. Now with ₹1,068 Cr revenue, margins are lower. This is consistent with a company buying revenue at below-cost pricing to win tenders — a common behavior among SMEs wanting to show "scale" pre and post-IPO.

**7. Auditor is Not Independent Enough:**
K C Parikh & Associates is a small Ahmedabad firm. For ₹1,000 Cr revenue with complex project accounting, the assurance level is materially lower than what a Big-4 or Tier-2 national firm would provide.

**8. The Working Capital Miracle is Suspicious:**
Cash Conversion Cycle went from 164 days (FY25) to 57 days (FY26) in one year — a 65% improvement while revenue tripled. In the EPC business, this kind of change either reflects: (a) a genuine shift to advance-payment from government clients (unusual), (b) change in revenue recognition assumptions, or (c) seasonal/year-end balance sheet window dressing (receivables collected late March, re-extended in Q1 FY27). Without quarterly cash flow data, this cannot be verified.

**Short-Seller Verdict:** GGBL is not clearly a fraud, but it exhibits 6 of 8 classic SME value-trap indicators: post-IPO institutional exit, accrual-heavy earnings, governance opacity, ambitious pivot to unproven business, SME illiquidity, and aggressive revenue growth with margin deterioration. A disciplined short-seller would be investigating further — particularly the BESS order specifics and Q1 FY27 working capital position.
