## 1. Executive Summary

**Verdict: AVOID** (for a ₹10,000 Cr long-only institutional mandate; see §16 for the nuanced sizing discussion)

**Conviction: 25/100** | **Risk: 80/100**

**Margin of safety: Negative to none at CMP.** At ~10x trailing P/E on a base of one hyper-growth year (FY26 revenue +185% YoY), off a tiny historical base, with negative operating cash flow in the very year profits tripled, there is no margin of safety — the multiple already prices continued flawless execution.

**Key thesis (bull case):** Alpex Solar rode India's ALMM-driven import-substitution wave in solar modules, growing standalone revenue from ₹404 Cr (FY24) to ₹2,218 Cr (FY26), a ~2.3x increase in one year, while expanding module capacity from 1.2 GW toward a targeted 3.6 GW and building a 2.2 GW TOPCon solar-cell plant that would make it a backward-integrated player ahead of the mandatory June-2026 ALMM List-II cell requirement. If cell integration lands as guided, management targets EBITDA margins of 29–30% (vs. current 14–16%), which — if achieved and sustained — would justify a much higher earnings base than today's.

**Key bear thesis:** The FY26 profit surge was **not accompanied by cash profit**. Standalone operating cash flow was **−₹117 Cr in FY26 against a reported net profit of ₹204 Cr** — a ₹320+ Cr divergence between accounting profit and cash generated, funded by a ₹553 Cr financing inflow (a ₹260 Cr preferential issue plus a roughly 6.8x increase in borrowings, from ₹79 Cr to ₹535–537 Cr in one year). Inventory days rose from 39 (FY24) to 73 (FY26). The company is simultaneously: (a) building three new plants on borrowed/raised capital, (b) reporting a return profile (ROE 52%, ROCE 44%) that is mechanically inflated by a tiny prior-year equity base, (c) operating in an industry every credible sell-side/rating-agency source flags as heading into **overcapacity** (India module manufacturing capacity ~125 GW vs. domestic demand of 40–50 GW/year), and (d) carrying an SME-board liquidity/disclosure discount with a promoter-entity share pledge on record. This is a leveraged, working-capital-hungry growth story in a sector approaching a capacity glut, priced as if none of that risk exists.

**3–5yr outlook:** Wide dispersion. Base case is single-digit-to-flat real equity returns as margin/multiple compression from sector overcapacity offsets volume growth; bull case requires (i) the cell plant to ramp on time and at guided margins, (ii) no industry price collapse, and (iii) no further dilutive/leveraged capital raises; bear case is a working-capital/liquidity event during a capex-heavy, cash-flow-negative expansion phase, compounded by SME-board illiquidity on the way down.

**Biggest unknowns:**
- Whether FY26's OCF shortfall is a one-off (heavy inventory build ahead of the cell-plant ramp) or a structural feature of the business model (Unknown — requires FY27 cash-flow statement to resolve).
- Whether Alpex has secured, or will secure, any PLI (Production-Linked Incentive) tranche allocation — no confirmation found that Alpex is among the 11 PLI Tranche-II awardees (Cannot be verified from available sources).

## 3. Industry & Sector

**Facts:**
- India's solar module manufacturing capacity is set to exceed **125 GW**, with **~29 GW of inventory** already building up — an overcapacity signal flagged by Wood Mackenzie. [Source](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/india-solar-supply-chain-growth-pain/)
- Domestic cell capacity is projected to reach **60 GW by FY27** against an **annual module requirement of only 40–50 GW**, per rating-agency commentary — implying reliance on exports to absorb excess. [ICRA](https://www.icra.in/CommonService/OpenMediaS3?Key=f2cf97b7-5ff8-4952-973d-3ce1ab99336c)
- **Module exports to the US fell 52% YoY in 1H2025**, closing the main export outlet just as domestic capacity surges — a direct threat to the "export the glut" escape valve. [Source cited in search synthesis, cross-verify at primary trade-data level — MEDIUM confidence]
- **ALMM List-II mandate for solar cells goes live June 2026**, forcing module makers to source ALMM-listed domestic cells — a genuine near-term tailwind for players (like Alpex) racing to get cell capacity ALMM-certified before the deadline, but a level playing field since most large peers are doing the same.
- SBI Capital has publicly flagged a possible Indian solar oversupply by 2027. [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/energy/indian-solar-sector-may-face-oversupply-by-2027-sbi-capital)
- PLI (Production-Linked Incentive) Tranche-II LoAs (39,600 MW aggregate) were issued to **11 bidders** in April 2023 — Alpex Solar's name was **not identified** in the sources reviewed as one of those 11 (Cannot be verified either way from available data — treat as **not a confirmed PLI beneficiary**).

**Inference:** The dominant sector narrative — "India solar manufacturing is a structural PLI/ALMM winner" — is a **2023–2025 story**. By 2026, the informed sell-side/rating-agency view has already rotated toward **overcapacity risk**, i.e., the easy re-rating window for undifferentiated module/cell capacity may have closed just as Alpex is committing its largest-ever capex.

**Porter's Five Forces (module/cell manufacturing):**
- Threat of new entrants: **HIGH** — capital and policy incentives have drawn dozens of new entrants (own view; corroborated by the capacity-glut data above).
- Supplier power (polysilicon/wafer, still largely China-linked until cell integration completes): **HIGH**.
- Buyer power (PSU/EPC tenders, L1 bidding): **HIGH** — government tenders are price-competitive by design.
- Threat of substitutes: **LOW** (solar PV itself is the substitute winning against other generation, but that's an industry-level not company-level tailwind).
- Rivalry: **VERY HIGH** — commoditized product, dozens of scaled domestic competitors, imminent overcapacity.

**Expected sector CAGR:** India's solar installation base is expected to keep growing at a healthy double-digit clip through the decade (broad consensus, not company-specific), but **manufacturer economics and installation growth are decoupling** — installation growth does not guarantee manufacturer margin/ROIC durability in an overcapacity market. **Confidence: MEDIUM** (macro demand figures are consensus view; overcapacity/margin-compression thesis is analytically inferred from the cited sources, not a single authoritative forecast).

## 10. Valuation

**A formal DCF is not meaningful here and is deliberately not presented as a primary output.** The company has: (1) only 2–3 years of a stable-enough business model to model forward, (2) currently negative free cash flow (−₹437 Cr FY26), (3) a capex program whose completion date and resulting margin profile are guidance, not fact, and (4) revenue growth (+185% YoY) so far outside any sustainable steady-state that terminal-value assumptions would dominate and mislead the output. Presenting a precise DCF number here would create false precision.

**Relative valuation (what is available):**
- CMP ₹800, TTM (FY26) EPS-derived P/E ≈ **10x** on a base year in which OCF was negative — i.e., the "cheap" 10x multiple is being paid on the **lowest-cash-quality** earnings year in the company's history. On a cash-earnings basis (if profit were normalized down toward operating cash flow), the effective multiple is far higher, arguably not meaningfully computable given OCF was negative.

## 15. Short-Seller Attack (assume value-trap / operator-driven SME pump until proven otherwise)

Building the strongest evidence-based bear case deliberately:

1. **Profit-cash divergence is the smoking gun.** ₹204 Cr reported net profit vs. −₹117 Cr operating cash flow in the same year is exactly the pattern a forensic short-seller would lead with. Combined with rising inventory days (39→73 over two years), the case that "reported growth is being manufactured or at minimum front-loaded via aggressive inventory/revenue recognition ahead of actual cash realization" is not paranoid — it is the single most defensible, data-supported bear argument in this entire report.
2. **Leverage exploded in lockstep with the profit surge.** Borrowings up ~6.9x in one year is not how a genuinely self-funding, high-ROCE business behaves — high-ROCE businesses throw off cash; Alpex's reported 44% ROCE coincided with the company needing ₹553 Cr of external financing just to stay afloat operationally *and* fund capex. That combination (headline-high ROCE + external-financing-dependent cash position) is a textbook divergence worth scrutinizing.
3. **Promoter-entity pledge, unresolved.** A promoter vehicle pledging over a million shares inside the same fiscal year the company is telling the market a growth story, with no clear public resolution found, is exactly the kind of disclosure a short-seller report would highlight — pledged shares create forced-selling risk on any price weakness, which can become self-reinforcing on a thin SME-board float.
4. **Dilution stacking.** Preferential issue (Sep-2025) + outstanding warrants (further dilution baked in on conversion) + equity capital already up from ₹24 Cr to ₹26 Cr — shareholders are being diluted at the same time reported per-share metrics (EPS, ROE) look spectacular, a combination that flatters trailing ratios while quietly eroding forward per-share economics.
5. **SME-board dynamics.** Fixed-lot trading, thin institutional ownership (FII 3.2%, DII 0.4%), a retail-dominated and rapidly growing shareholder base (2,224→8,032 holders in two years), and a >2x realized 52-week trading range are all consistent with the classic SME "story stock" pattern — price discovery driven more by narrative/momentum than by institutional fundamental scrutiny, which is precisely the environment in which "fluff order announcements" (real orders, but announced with promotional framing/timing relative to price action) tend to have outsized, temporary price impact.
6. **No independent confirmation of PLI status, export mix, RPT quantum, or current statutory auditor identity** — for a company this small on an SME board, the *absence* of easily obtainable answers to basic diligence questions is itself informative; a mainboard peer of comparable narrative strength would have these facts readily available via sell-side coverage.
7. **Counter-evidence (steelmanning the bull side, for balance):** the government order book (SECI, CMPDI) is real and verifiable, not vaporware; the preferential-issue investor base is named/institutional, not obviously promoter-linked shell entities; debtor days actually *improved*, arguing against a pure receivables-based revenue-inflation scheme; and the capex is visibly real (physical plants under construction, multiple independent trade-press site visits/reports). **This is not a "zero product, pure promotion" pump — it is a real, operating, fast-growing manufacturer with a genuinely weak cash-conversion profile during its heaviest-ever investment year.** The fair characterization is "high-risk, cash-flow-negative growth story with real assets and real orders, priced without adequate discount for its earnings-quality and sector-timing risk" rather than "outright fraud."

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** that the cash-flow/leverage pattern described is a legitimate, material risk (it is directly computable from disclosed numbers); **LOW-MEDIUM** on any implication of intentional manipulation, which is not evidenced and is explicitly not alleged here — the forensic read supports "weak earnings quality," not "fraud."

## 16. Investment Committee Decision

**Recommendation: DO NOT ALLOCATE at this time. Target allocation: 0%.**

For a ₹10,000 Cr long-only mandate, this name fails on multiple independent, non-overlapping grounds before valuation is even the deciding factor:

1. **Structural fit:** SME-board liquidity almost certainly falls outside most institutional mandates' investable universe on ADTV/free-float grounds alone — a ₹10,000 Cr fund attempting any meaningful position size would itself move the price and likely breach practical position-sizing/liquidity limits (own analytical judgment, given the negligible FII/DII holding already observed in the actual float).
2. **Earnings quality:** The one reliably computable forensic signal (accrual ratio, OCF-vs-PAT divergence) is clearly negative in the most recent fiscal year — this alone would warrant a pass or, at most, a watch-list designation pending resolution, under any disciplined quality-first process.
3. **Sector timing:** Committing capital into a capital-intensive expansion at the exact moment independent, credible sources (Wood Mackenzie, ICRA, SBI Capital) are flagging sector overcapacity is contrarian in the wrong direction — buying the marginal, smallest-scale, most-levered capacity addition into a glut is a weak risk/reward setup.
4. **Governance:** An unresolved promoter-entity pledge and unquantified RPT exposure are exactly the items a governance-conscious institutional process screens out pre-emptively, not items to underwrite through.

**What would need to change to revisit:**
- Confirmed migration to mainboard listing (resolves the structural liquidity disqualification).
- At least two consecutive quarters of **positive operating cash flow** that converges toward reported net profit (resolves the earnings-quality question).
- Full, clean resolution/explanation of the promoter pledge, with confirmation of no further pledge escalation.
- Confirmed PLI status (or a credible cost-competitiveness case in its absence) and a clearer picture of RPTs and statutory-auditor identity/tenure.
- Evidence that the cell-plant ramp is delivering toward the 29–30% EBITDA-margin guidance in realized (not guided) numbers, ideally with margin improving *before* the next major capex cycle rather than the current pattern of margin declining through the growth phase.

Until then, this is appropriately a **name to track on a watchlist, not to own** at institutional scale.
