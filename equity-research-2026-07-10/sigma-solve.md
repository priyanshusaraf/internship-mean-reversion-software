## 1. Executive Summary

**Verdict: AVOID**
**Conviction: 72/100 (Avoid)**
**Risk: 78/100**
**Margin of Safety: NEGATIVE — stock appears fairly to moderately overvalued vs. decelerating growth**

### Key Thesis (Bull Case)
Sigma Solve is a genuinely profitable, nearly debt-free SME IT services company with a verified 5-year revenue CAGR of ~35–36%, net profit CAGR of ~50%, ROE consistently above 30%, ROCE above 48%, and clean cash flow conversion (OCF > PAT in FY26). The business has organically grown from ₹10 Cr to ₹96 Cr revenue in 6 years. At ~16.6x trailing PE, it trades at a discount to large-cap IT peers on earnings multiple.

### Key Bear Thesis (Why It Fails Due Diligence)
1. **Structural RPT risk is the dominant concern.** ~100% of revenue is channeled through Sigma Solve Inc. (a now wholly-owned subsidiary incorporated in Florida, USA). The Indian listed entity essentially functions as a captive delivery arm billing its US subsidiary on a man-hour basis. This is not a conventional arm's-length revenue model — it is a related-party-mediated revenue structure. Revenue "quality" and "independence" cannot be verified without US subsidiary audited accounts.
2. **Revenue concentration = 100% single-entity RPT.** There is no external customer diversification at the listed-entity level. Revenue could be adjusted, smoothed, or understated/overstated via intercompany pricing without detection from Indian filings alone.
3. **SEBI has taken enforcement action twice (Aug 2025, Sep 2025).** One order concerns trading activities in the scrip; one concerns IPO proceeds misuse. The trading activities order is a material red flag for potential market manipulation (pump). Details of the Aug 2025 order (trading activities) are not publicly accessible in full, which itself is a transparency concern.
4. **Multiple MCA director defaults (20) and bank defaults (19) flagged** by company check databases — governance quality is a legitimate concern.
5. **Growth is decelerating sharply.** Revenue CAGR was 36–42% over 5 years; Q4 FY26 revenue grew only ~4.7% YoY with profit DOWN 20-21% YoY. The deceleration is abrupt and unexplained in public disclosures.
6. **SME liquidity is thin.** Daily traded value on combined NSE+BSE is ~₹35–40 lakh. At ₹396 Cr market cap, exit is difficult for any institutional-sized position.
7. **Governance complexity:** company has changed its legal name multiple times (at least 5 prior names), has GSTIN cancellations in 2 states, litigation in district and high courts (35+45 cases), and 94 employees for ₹96 Cr revenue.

### 3–5 Year Outlook
**Conditional:** IF the RPT structure is legitimate, IF US subsidiary revenues are genuinely from third-party clients, and IF growth re-accelerates to 15–20%, the company could reach ₹150–180 Cr revenue by FY30 with ~₹35–40 Cr PAT. IF the structure is operated unfairly or revenues are inflated, downside is severe (>70% capital loss). The structural ambiguity makes forecasting unreliable.

### Biggest Unknowns
1. Actual end-client concentration and identity (US subsidiary is the only "client")
2. Whether US subsidiary has independent third-party revenues or is a pass-through
3. Full text of Aug 2025 SEBI trading activities order
4. Why Q4 FY26 profit fell 20% YoY despite 4.7% revenue growth
5. Working capital days expansion to ~69–100 days — receivables from whom?

**Confidence in this verdict: MEDIUM-HIGH** (structural red flags are verifiable; financial performance data is consistent across multiple sources)

## 15. Short-Seller Attack

**Hypothesis: Sigma Solve Ltd is a related-party-mediated earnings stream with opaque US operations, subject to SEBI scrutiny, operating in a commodity IT services niche with no verifiable independent customer base.**

### Evidence-Based Bear Case

1. **The "client" is the company itself.** Sigma Solve Ltd's only disclosed revenue source is Sigma Solve Inc., which Sigma Solve Ltd owns 100%. The MD/promoter (Prakash Parikh) owns both. Revenue can be set at any level via intercompany pricing. There is no independent audit trail from US client contracts in Indian filings. This is structurally identical to how inflated revenues are manufactured in SME frauds.

2. **Receivables ballooning = possible smoothing.** Debtor days went from 29 (FY22) → 82 (FY24) → 100 (FY25) → 70 (FY26). The "debtors" are the US subsidiary. The Indian entity books revenue; the US subsidiary doesn't pay for 3 months. Then it pays, and the Indian entity records good cash flow. This creates a mechanism to manage OCF across periods.

3. **SEBI Aug 2025 trading order.** SEBI investigated "trading activities in the scrip" — not the company itself. This suggests SEBI examined secondary market trading patterns. In SME stocks, this typically means examining whether operators/promoter associates were ramping or dumping the stock. The fact that an order was issued (not just investigated) suggests SEBI found something.

4. **Promoter didn't sell at ₹65 peak?** Promoter holding has stayed at 73.22% through the 52-week range from ₹35 to ₹65. If this is legitimate, promoters have conviction.
