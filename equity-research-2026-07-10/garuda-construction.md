## 1. Executive Summary

**Verdict: Avoid** (institutional long-only fund; not a "never touch," but does not clear a bar for meaningful capital allocation today).

**Conviction (on the Avoid call): 62/100**
**Risk score: 74/100** (high — small-cap, concentrated, RPT-heavy, promoter-litigation flagged, working-capital history erratic)
**Margin of safety: LOW-MEDIUM.** Headline P/E (~13.5x) looks cheap vs peers (21–36x at IPO time), but the multiple discount is a rational market response to earnings-quality and governance questions below, not a mispricing.

**Key thesis (bull case):** Garuda is a capital-light, high-ROE (31%), high-ROCE (42%), near-debt-free civil-construction EPC contractor riding Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR) redevelopment and real-estate construction demand. Revenue has compounded ~49% over 5 years, order book has scaled to multi-billion-rupee levels (~₹3,460–4,877cr depending on the reporting date), and FY26 standalone revenue more than doubled YoY (₹225cr → ₹531cr) with margins holding at ~31% OPM. It trades at a discount to listed EPC peers (PSP Projects, Ahluwalia Contracts, Capacite Infraprojects) despite superior return ratios.

**Key bear thesis:** A large share of historical revenue and order book has been **related-party** business originating from group entities controlled by the same promoter (Pravin Kumar Brijendra Kumar Agarwal, who is also CMD of promoter/OFS-seller PKH Ventures Ltd) — 87–95% of revenue in FY22–FY23, still ~35% of the current order book. Receivables have historically ballooned to extreme levels (417 debtor-days in FY24), operating cash flow was **negative ₹111cr in FY25 against a reported PAT of ₹50cr** (a severe cash-earnings divergence), and the promoter faces a pending criminal case (IPC rioting/hurt sections). This is a classic profile where reported growth and margins may be partly a function of intra-group contracting rather than arm's-length market competitiveness — a "quasi-captive EPC arm" risk that institutional investors should discount heavily until multiple years of clean, non-related-party, cash-converting growth are demonstrated.

**3–5yr outlook:** Conditional. If MMR redevelopment demand stays strong AND related-party revenue share keeps falling AND receivables/OCF normalize, the growth story could be legitimate and re-rate. If related-party dependency persists or reverses, or if working capital deteriorates again, this is a value-trap candidate.

**Biggest unknowns:** True arm's-length pricing on related-party contracts; sustainability of FY26 margin (31%) at 4-5x the FY24 revenue base; auditor identity/opinion history (not independently confirmed); outcome of the pending criminal matter and its reputational/governance read-through; whether the FY25 OCF collapse (-₹111cr) was a one-off working-capital timing issue or a recurring pattern (FY26 OCF recovered to +₹41cr, one data point only).

Confidence: MEDIUM — data triangulated across screener.in, IPO research notes, and news aggregators; no primary DRHP/annual-report/credit-rating-rationale document was directly fetched (see §6, §15 for what could not be verified).

## 3. Industry & Sector

**Facts:** India's real-estate and construction sector is a multi-decade structural growth story tied to urbanization, MMR redevelopment policy (cluster/cessed-building redevelopment schemes), and continued residential demand in Mumbai. Government infra capex (roads, railways, urban infra) is a broader tailwind but Garuda's own disclosed exposure is overwhelmingly private residential/commercial EPC, not government infra contracting — so PLI/defense/railway policy narratives are **not directly relevant to this company** and are excluded per instructions.

**Demand drivers (Inference/Opinion):** MMR redevelopment pipeline (cessed buildings, SRA, cluster redevelopment) is large and multi-year; a construction-cost-inflation tailwind benefits EPC revenue in rupee terms but can compress margins if input costs (steel, cement, labor) outpace contract escalation clauses.

**Commodity exposure:** Steel and cement cost inflation is a real risk to EPC margins; Unknown whether Garuda's contracts carry price-escalation clauses — Requires further investigation.

**Cyclicality:** High. Construction/real-estate is a classic late-cycle, credit-sensitive, interest-rate-sensitive sector. A residential real estate slowdown in MMR would directly hit both the group's developer side and Garuda's contracting revenue simultaneously (correlated, not diversifying, risk).

**Porter's Five Forces (Opinion):**
- Rivalry: High (PSP Projects, Ahluwalia, Capacite, Vascon, B L Kashyap all compete regionally/nationally).
- Supplier power: Moderate (labor and material suppliers are fragmented).
- Buyer power: High for open-market clients; Low/structural for related-party clients (which is itself the concern).
- Threat of new entrants: Moderate — capital-light but requires track record/relationships for large redevelopment contracts.
- Substitutes: Low (construction cannot be substituted, but market share can shift to competitors).

**Expected sector CAGR:** Indian real-estate/construction sector broadly forecast at low-to-mid teens CAGR by most industry trackers (Opinion/Forecast, not independently sourced for this report — Requires further investigation for a citable figure).

Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM — sector view is directional, not sourced to a specific market-research report in this pass.

## 13. SWOT

**Strengths:** Near-zero debt; high ROE/ROCE (31%/42%) vs peers; capital-light EPC model; multi-year MMR redevelopment relationships; fast revenue growth (49% 5yr CAGR).

**Weaknesses:** Extreme historical related-party revenue dependency (up to 95% of sales in FY23); OCF materially below PAT in 3 of last 4 years; historically extreme debtor days (417 in FY24); zero dividend despite reported profitability; auditor identity/opinion history not confirmed in this review.

**Opportunities:** MMR redevelopment pipeline (SRA/cluster/cessed-building schemes) is large and structurally multi-year; order book (₹3,460–4,877cr range disclosed) provides revenue visibility if execution and cash conversion normalize; potential re-rating toward peer multiples (21–36x) if related-party share keeps falling and OCF quality improves.

## 15. Short-Seller Attack (assume value-trap / operator-driven thesis)

Building the strongest evidence-based bear case deliberately, as instructed:

1. **The company is substantially a captive construction arm of its own promoter's real-estate group.** FY22 (87%), FY23 (95%), and the Apr-2024 stub period (100%) of revenue came from related parties. A "third-party, competitively-won EPC contractor" narrative used to justify a re-rating toward peers like Ahluwalia or PSP Projects is not fully supported by the disclosed revenue mix history — a meaningful part of Garuda's business is the promoter selling contracts to himself, on both sides of the transaction (PKH Ventures/Makindian Township as developer, Garuda as contractor).
2. **The 30%+ EBITDA margins that make Garuda look like the "best-in-class" peer may simply reflect intra-group pricing, not competitive execution excellence.** Margins this high are atypical for arm's-length EPC subcontracting; a related-party counterparty has no incentive to negotiate the contractor down on price.
3. **Receivables and cash conversion tell a starkly different story than the P&L.** 417 debtor-days in FY24, ₹176cr receivables against ₹154cr revenue, ₹58cr overdue beyond 6 months, and a **-₹111cr operating cash outflow in FY25 against a reported ₹50cr profit** — this is receivable-funded (or at minimum receivable-delayed) revenue recognition, a classic short-seller target pattern (revenue/profit booked, cash not collected).
4. **Promoter faces a pending criminal case** (IPC 143/147/149/325 — unlawful assembly, rioting, causing hurt) — while unrelated to financial fraud per se, it is a governance/reputational overhang that a short-seller report would highlight prominently.
5. **The same promoter's other listed vehicle, PKH Ventures, is reported to have had a prior IPO subscription/withdrawal issue** — worth flagging as a pattern-of-behavior data point, though this specific claim should be independently re-verified before being treated as fully confirmed (Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM on this specific sub-claim; flagged for further investigation).
6. **FII holding has fallen by more than half (as a % of float) in five reported quarters** (6.65%→2.64%), while retail shareholder count has risen ~12%. This divergence — smart money exiting while retail count grows — is exactly the shape a short-seller would present as evidence of distribution into retail demand, though it is also consistent with benign small-cap index rebalancing and is not proof of a pump.
7. **Zero dividends despite strong reported profitability and a near-debt-free balance sheet** is unusual for a company that claims strong, clean cash generation — if the cash were really there and clean, a maiden dividend post-IPO would be a low-cost credibility signal; its absence is at minimum neutral-to-negative evidence, not a red flag on its own but consistent with the broader cash-quality skepticism above.
8. **2026 share price underperformance** (-27% over 6 months, -12.9% YTD vs Sensex -9.8%) after a strong 2025 rally is consistent with the market progressively re-pricing these concerns as more post-IPO quarters of data have become available — i.e., the multiple compression from peer-like levels toward the current 13.5x may itself be the market's verdict on the RPT/cash-quality question, already partially in motion.

**Counter-evidence to be fair (not just steel-manning the bear case):** related-party revenue share has fallen materially from FY23's 95% to an order-book share of ~35% now; debtor days improved from 417 to 165; OCF turned positive in FY26 (+₹41cr); promoter holding has not been sold down post-IPO beyond the original OFS; the business is asset-light and near-debt-free, limiting balance-sheet blow-up risk even in a bad scenario. This is a genuinely two-sided story, not a slam-dunk short thesis — but the evidence above is more than sufficient to keep this outside "core long-only allocation" territory today.

Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH on the facts cited; the overall short-thesis synthesis is Opinion built on those facts.
