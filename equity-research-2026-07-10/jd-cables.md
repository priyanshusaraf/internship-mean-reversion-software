## 1. Executive Summary

**Verdict: HOLD (leaning AVOID for institutional mandates)**
**Conviction: 28/100**
**Risk: 72/100**

### Key Thesis
JD Cables is a West Bengal-based manufacturer of aluminum cables, conductors (AAC/AAAC/ACSR), and power cables serving state electricity boards across 10+ Indian states. It has ridden the India power infrastructure supercycle — revenue scaled from ₹40 Cr (FY23) to ₹365 Cr (FY26), a 3-year CAGR of ~108%. At trailing 17x PE and ₹550 Cr market cap, it appears cheap relative to the growth rate. The ₹515 Cr order book (1.4x revenue) provides near-term visibility. Government-mandated T&D investment, Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS), and PM Surya Ghar provide genuine tailwinds.

### Key Bear Thesis
The bear case is structurally compelling: (1) **Persistently negative operating cash flows** (-₹74 Cr OCF on +₹32 Cr PAT in FY26) signal a profitability-on-paper, cash-burn-in-reality business — a classic SME red flag. (2) **EPC pivot is a business model transformation risk** — the ₹244 Cr highway EPC project takes JD Cables into construction contracting, a sector with lumpier revenues, retention money, and balance sheet stress. (3) **Working capital deterioration is severe** — debtor days 96, inventory days 101, cash conversion cycle 168 days, up from ~49 days. (4) **Tiny promoter track record** — company founded 2015, promoter Piyush Garodia has ~10 years experience; no large-cap validation. (5) **SME board illiquidity** — lot size 800 shares (~₹1.95 lakh); not accessible to most institutional mandates. (6) **Single manufacturing concentration** — West Bengal; no geographic diversification in production.

### Margin of Safety
Insufficient. At 17x trailing PE on a company with negative free cash flow and a business model pivot to EPC, there is no margin of safety. Intrinsic value under base case: ₹175–220/share; current price ₹243 implies modest overvaluation.

### 3–5 Year Outlook
If EPC execution succeeds and OCF turns positive, FY27–28 earnings could reach ₹60–80 Cr (45–55 Cr for cables + 15–25 Cr EPC contribution). That implies 8–9x forward PE — optically attractive. But EPC-linked working capital and execution risk make this a wide-distribution outcome. Bear case: EPC delays, receivables blowout, equity dilution → stock at ₹100 or below.

### Biggest Unknowns
1. EPC business execution — can a cable manufacturer profitably complete highway construction?
2. OCF trajectory — will working capital normalize or worsen as EPC scales?
3. Commodity pass-through — aluminum is volatile; can margins hold at 13%?
4. Customer concentration — state electricity board exposure is opaque; single client risk not quantified.
5. Auditor quality — M/s. Vinod Singhal & Co. (small regional CA firm); Big 4 equivalent validation absent.

## 8. Government Exposure

JD Cables is a **pure-play government-dependent business.** Estimated 80–90% of revenue comes from state electricity boards and government infrastructure projects (including EPC highway work).

**Direct links:**
- **RDSS (Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme):** ₹3.03 lakh crore outlay (2021–2026), extended; direct conductor/cable demand for last-mile distribution.
- **PMSGY / PM Surya Ghar:** Aerial bunched cable demand for rooftop solar connectivity.
- **NHDP Phase V (National Highway projects):** EPC order for NH-2 Jharkhand-Bihar six-laning — subcontract via Kaushal Engineering Limited (not direct NHAI award, which adds counter-party risk).
- **Northeast infrastructure push:** Direct state-level T&D projects.
- **Import substitution:** No large import threat for conductors (heavy commodity items); government preference for domestic sourcing.

**Risk:** Government capex cycles can slow due to elections, budget compression, or policy change. SEB payment delays are chronic in some states (notably Bihar, Jharkhand — where JD Cables is gaining exposure). EPC being awarded via sub-contracting (not directly from NHAI) adds an additional layer of credit/execution risk.

## 15. Short-Seller Attack Case

*Assume worst-case thesis. Falsify before accepting.*

### Attack Points (evidence-based):

1. **Revenue is accounting income, not cash.** Three consecutive years of negative OCF while reporting growing profits. Every year "profit" exists on paper; no cash arrives. This is the textbook sign of earnings being pulled forward through receivables (revenue recognized before collection). The FY26 OCF of -₹74 Cr vs. PAT of +₹32 Cr is a ₹106 Cr divergence. Where did the money go? Into 168-day cash conversion cycle.

2. **The EPC pivot is a distraction / revenue smoothing mechanism.** EPC business allows milestone-based revenue recognition. If cable business slows, EPC revenue can be booked on percentage-completion basis even before cash arrives. The ₹407 Cr highway project provides 12+ months of booking opportunity. Whether this reflects genuine value creation or revenue management requires full audit scrutiny.

3. **Auditor is not credible for institutional analysis.** M/s. Vinod Singhal & Co. is a small CA firm. There is no known track record of auditing listed companies of this size with EPC exposure. The large-cap cable companies use Deloitte, KPMG, or BSR (KPMG affiliate). Quality of earnings validation is uncertain.

4. **Promoter monetized at IPO.** While OFS of ₹11.58 Cr is modest, promoter who held 97% now at 70% — post lock-up expiry (typically 3 years for promoters, but 1 year for pre-IPO investors), secondary selling is a risk. The IPO was priced at ₹152; stock reached ₹251 — promoter has a 65% profit incentive to sell.

5. **Customer concentration undisclosed.** Despite SEC-equivalent filing requirements in India, the names of top 10 customers are not available in public sources. This is a serious governance gap for an ₹365 Cr revenue company. SEB concentration creates singular political/payment risk.

6. **EPC counter-party risk.** The ₹244 Cr highway order is a subcontract from Kaushal Engineering Limited (not a direct NHAI award). If Kaushal Engineering faces financial stress or project delays, JD Cables as sub-contractor bears the cost risk without direct government backing. This adds counter-party credit risk.

7. **127x IPO subscription ≠ quality.** SME IPOs with 100x+ subscription are common in India's retail-driven market. High subscription in SME IPOs often reflects grey market premium speculation, not fundamental analysis. Stock listed at only 5% premium despite 127x subscription — the market found the offering expensive.

8. **Management experience gap.** CEO is ~37 years old (founded company in 2015 at ~26), managing a company now entering EPC highway construction, T&D line installation, and solar EPC. This is a dramatic expansion of business complexity for a team with no disclosed large-infrastructure background.

9. **Working capital cycle shows no signs of improvement.** FY26 working capital days (113) doubled from FY24–25 (49). If EPC scales to ₹200 Cr in FY27, the milestone-based billing and retention money will likely push working capital days beyond 130–150 days. This requires either bank credit (adding debt) or equity dilution.

10. **No peer on BSE SME disclosed.** The IPO prospectus states "No listed peers available for comparison" — making valuation entirely discretionary, with risk of manipulation of comparable multiples by promoters/lead managers.
